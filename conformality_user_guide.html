Conformality User Guide

Conformality is our in-house tool for proving that we know who our suppliers and customers are, what we sell, and where it goes. It collects evidence, flags problems, records who decided what, and keeps the history so it can be shown to an auditor or customer.

Important. This tool organizes work and records decisions. It does not give legal advice and it does not decide whether an export licence is required. When a result is unclear, or a match is confirmed, involve your compliance lead or legal counsel.

Getting around

The top bar has one tab per area. Colored badges show status: green is fine, amber needs a look soon, red needs action, gray is neutral or not started. Most actions open a pop-up window. Decisions that matter ask you to type a short reason, and that reason is saved permanently.

A typical first week

  1. Minerals: load the smelter list (RMAP).
  2. Screening: load the screening list from trade.gov.
  3. Suppliers and SKUs: add suppliers, one contact each, and import your part numbers.
  4. Requests: ask each supplier for their conflict minerals form.
  5. Trade: classify your products and add your customers.
  6. Today: work through whatever it lists.

Actions

  • + Add supplier creates a supplier.
  • Manage contact opens the supplier's single contact. If one exists the form is pre-filled for editing; if not it is blank. A supplier can only be asked for documents once it has a contact.
  • Request documents starts a request, explained under Requests.

SKUs

PurposeOur product catalogue. A SKU (stock keeping unit) is one sellable part number. Linking each SKU to its supplier lets us answer a customer's question "is part X conflict-minerals compliant?" by looking up the supplier's current form.
  • Search by SKU, description or supplier.
  • + Add SKU adds one part.
  • Import CSV adds many. Columns: sku and supplier (required, supplier name must match exactly), plus optional description and supplier part number. Existing SKUs are updated, not duplicated. Rows with an unrecognized supplier are skipped and listed.

Requests

PurposeTracks every document we have asked a supplier for, so nothing is forgotten and there is a record that we asked.
Suppliers → Request documents→App creates a private upload link→You email the link→Supplier uploads→You review it
  • Creating a request logs it as sent and generates a private link for the supplier. The app does not send email itself. It opens a pre-written email draft in your mail program, or you can copy the link. The link is shown only once, so copy or send it right away.
  • Remind makes a fresh link and a reminder email draft.
  • Close ends a request with an outcome (fulfilled, not applicable, supplier declined, no response escalated, other). Anything other than "fulfilled" needs a note.
  • Status: Sent, Reminded, Overdue (past the due date), Received.

Minerals

PurposeReads each supplier's conflict minerals form and checks every smelter on it against the official conformant list, so you only have to look at the exceptions.

Background in three sentences

Tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold (3TG) can come from mines that fund armed groups. Customers expect us to show our suppliers use responsible smelters. A CMRT is the standard spreadsheet suppliers fill in to list their smelters, and the RMAP list, published by the Responsible Minerals Initiative (RMI), says which smelters have passed an independent audit.

The page, top to bottom

  1. RMAP smelter list. Shows when it was last loaded. Download the latest Smelter Reference List from RMI and click Load newer list. Do this a few times a year, and before the annual customer reporting cycle.
  2. CMRTs waiting to be read. Forms suppliers have uploaded but the app has not yet read. Click Read & check smelters. The app extracts the smelter table and compares each one to the list.
  3. Checked declarations. Defaults to Needs attention. Switch to All to see past years, filter by year or supplier, and optionally show archived items.

Smelter results

ResultMeaning
ConformantPassed audit. Nothing to do.
On RMI list, conformance not statedKnown smelter; the form does not state its audit status.
Active (in audit)Currently going through an audit.
Non-conformantFailed audit or was removed.
Not on RMAP listThe smelter ID is not in our loaded list. Check the spelling first, then the list date.
No smelter IDThe supplier left the ID blank.

Reviewing a declaration

Click Review on a row. You see the problem smelters. For each, record a decision with a short note:

  • Accepted after review – you looked and are comfortable.
  • Following up with supplier – waiting for an answer.
  • Supplier confirmed not in use – the supplier says that smelter is not actually in our supply chain.

When no flagged smelters remain open, the related case closes automatically. You can also add a note to any single smelter. Export the issues to CSV to send to the supplier.

The More ▾ menu offers: all declared smelters, re-check against a newer list, re-read the original file, and archive (with a reason) or restore.

After a document is checked, go to Review to accept, accept with gaps, or reject the document itself. Checking smelters and accepting the document are two separate decisions.

Screening

PurposeChecks the names of our suppliers and customers against government lists of sanctioned or restricted parties. Doing business with a listed party can lead to serious penalties, so we check regularly and keep proof that we did.

The lists

Use the free Consolidated Screening List (CSL) from trade.gov. It combines lists from three US agencies:

ListPublished byWhat it covers
OFAC SDN and non-SDNTreasury (Office of Foreign Assets Control)Sanctioned people, companies and vessels. Dealing with an SDN is generally prohibited.
BIS Entity List, Denied Persons, Military End-User, UnverifiedCommerce (Bureau of Industry and Security)Parties with export restrictions or extra licensing requirements.
State AECA DebarredState DepartmentParties barred from defense-related trade.

Loading a list

  1. On trade.gov, download the Consolidated Screening List as CSV.
  2. Click Load screening list (or Load newer list) and choose the file.
  3. The app shows a preview of how many entries were added, changed or removed. Confirm to apply it. A load that would remove an unusually large share of entries is refused unless you explicitly force it, which guards against loading a damaged file.

The original file and its fingerprint are saved as evidence of what the list said that day. The page warns Out of date after 14 days. Reload every one to two weeks.

Screening parties

  • Screen all parties checks everyone. Screen now on a row checks just that party.
  • The Parties table shows last screened date and status: Clear, n to review, Confirmed match, Not screened. "Re-screen due" appears after 30 days.
  • The app will not report a party as clear if no list is loaded.

Reviewing a possible match

Names are compared by similarity, so a hit is a possibility, not an accusation. Most are coincidences, such as a common name. Click Review to see your party next to the listed entry (country, address, aliases, program, remarks), then choose:

DecisionEffect
Not the same party (clear it)Recorded with your note. The same pair will not alert again unless the list entry itself changes.
Same party (confirm)Marked as a confirmed match. A red banner appears on the page.

A written reason is required for both. Example: "Different country and address; the listed party is an individual and ours is a company."

If you confirm a match: stop shipments and payments involving that party, and escalate to counsel. The app records the decision; it does not handle the legal response.

Trade

PurposeExport and import compliance. Before a product leaves the country we need to know how it is controlled; before an order ships we need to know who receives it and where. For imports we need the tariff code and country of origin so duties are right.

Key ideas

TermPlain meaning
EARExport Administration Regulations, run by Commerce. Covers most commercial products, including "dual-use" items with civilian and military uses. Controlled items have an ECCN code such as 7A994. Items with no specific control are EAR99, the lowest level.
ITARInternational Traffic in Arms Regulations, run by State. Covers items made specifically for military use, listed on the USML. Much stricter. The app only detects and holds ITAR items; it does not manage ITAR licensing.
JurisdictionWhich regime governs the item: EAR, ITAR, EU dual-use, or other. Decide this first.
HTS codeHarmonized Tariff Schedule number used by customs to set the duty on imports.
Country of originWhere the product was made, for customs and tariff purposes.

The Trade page has five sub-tabs.

1. Classification

PurposeRecords the export-control and customs classification for each product, with who decided it, how, and when.
  • Counters: active SKUs, unclassified, drafts to approve, reviews due, ITAR items. Filter by status or search by SKU, supplier, ECCN or HTS.
  • Classify opens a form: jurisdiction, source of the determination (supplier stated, a formal BIS ruling called a CCATS, self-classified, consultant), ECCN or USML category, encryption and GNSS (satellite positioning) flags, HTS code, country of origin, duty notes, applicable tariff programs, an optional evidence document, a rationale, and a next-review date.
  • Save draft keeps it for later. Save and approve finalizes it. Approval requires jurisdiction, a rationale and a source. EAR needs an ECCN (or EAR99). ITAR needs a USML category and can only be approved by an admin.
  • An approved classification cannot be edited. A change creates a new version, and History shows every version. This is deliberate: the history is your evidence.
  • Where the supplier gave you a classification, record them as Supplier stated. It is still our responsibility, so someone should sanity-check it.

Importing classifications (CSV). Required column sku; optional jurisdiction, eccn, usml, hts, origin, source, rationale. Imported rows always become drafts; you still review and approve each one. Importing the same file twice creates duplicate drafts.

2. Orders

PurposeThe pre-shipment check. Each order's parties, destination and items are screened, and a person records the decision.
  1. + New order: enter the order number, date, destination country (two letters), the customer, optional ship-to, end user, consignee and freight forwarder, and the items with quantities.
  2. Screen order runs the checks and sets a status: Cleared, On hold, or stays Not screened.
  3. If on hold, read the reasons, resolve them, then Release… (with a note) or Block….
Puts the order on holdShown as a note ("check before shipping")
  • No destination
  • Destination is embargoed or restricted (Destinations tab)
  • A party has an open or confirmed list match
  • No items on the order
  • An item has no approved classification
  • An item is ITAR
  • Destination is on the watch list
  • Item has a control other than EAR99
  • EU or other jurisdiction
  • Encryption or GNSS involved

An order with a confirmed or open list match cannot be released. ITAR orders can only be released by an admin. Re-screen after any change.

Screening an order does not decide whether a licence is required. Notes such as "non-EAR99" are prompts to check with your compliance lead or freight forwarder before shipping.

3. Customers & ship-to

PurposeThe list of non-supplier parties: customers, ship-to addresses, end users, consignees and freight forwarders. They need to exist here so they can be put on orders and screened.

+ Add party: name, company or individual, country, one or more roles, address, and whether it is publicly traded. The end user is whoever finally uses the product, which may differ from the buyer. This matters for export rules. Deactivate a party instead of deleting it, so history is kept. Suppliers are managed on the Suppliers tab.

4. Customs records

PurposeA filing cabinet for import and export paperwork, with automatic retention dates. Customs authorities can ask for these documents years later.

+ Upload record. Types: entry summary, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, export filing, broker file, export licence. Optionally link it to an order. The keep until date is calculated from the document date using the retention period (default five years, an admin setting). Files cannot be changed or deleted once stored; each is fingerprinted.

5. Destinations

PurposeA list of countries that trigger a hold or a note when an order is screened.

Levels: Embargoed and Restricted put orders on hold; Watch adds a note only. The list starts empty on purpose: your admin and counsel decide what belongs, so the app never implies comfort from a default it guessed. Only an admin can add or remove rules. Until rules are set, orders to any destination are not held on country grounds.

Review

PurposeThe inbox for everything suppliers send us. Nothing a supplier uploads counts until a person accepts it.

Documents to review

Each uploaded document shows the supplier, type, upload date and a SHA-256 "fingerprint" that proves the file has not changed since upload. For conflict minerals forms it also shows how many smelters need attention. Buttons: View, Open file, and then one decision:

DecisionUse when
AcceptThe document is complete and usable.
Accept with gapsUsable but incomplete. A note records what is missing.
RejectUnusable. The supplier will need to send another.

Ownership submissions

Suppliers can certify who owns them. Ownership matters because sanctions rules (for example OFAC's "50 percent rule") can apply to a company owned by listed parties even when the company itself is not listed. Review the named owners, then Accept & screen owners (the owners are added and screened) or Send back.

Audit

PurposeThe permanent, tamper-evident record of everything done in the app. This is what you show an auditor or customer to prove the process is real.
  • Each entry shows number, time, who, whether something was created, changed or deleted, which record, and what changed. Details shows before and after values.
  • Filter by date, area (such as Screening hit or Classification), action, or free text.
  • The badge at the top (Integrity verified) means the chain of entries checks out. Integrity problem means an entry was altered or removed, so tell your admin immediately.
  • Export CSV for the full set; Print / save as PDF report for a formatted report. The screen shows the latest 500 entries.
  • Entries cannot be edited or deleted by anyone through the app.

The supplier portal (what suppliers see)

PurposeA simple page where suppliers upload what we asked for, without needing an account or password.
  • The supplier opens the private link you sent, sees what is requested, and uploads the file. Some requests also ask them to certify ownership.
  • Links expire. Each is tied to one supplier and one request. If a link expires or is lost, use Remind on Requests to make a fresh one.
  • Uploads appear in Review. Suppliers see only their own request, never other data in the app.

Notes for the administrator

  • Roles. Users have a role on their profile. Admins can approve ITAR classifications, release ITAR orders, and edit Destination rules. Other users can read and perform the routine work. Role names are shown top right next to your name.
  • Settings (stored in the database) include screening match thresholds, how many days before the list is "out of date" (14), re-screening interval (30 days), and customs retention years (5). Change these with your compliance lead, not casually.
  • Protected data. Screening lists, audit entries and approved classifications are write-protected. They can only change through the app's built-in actions, which is why the history can be trusted.
  • Setup. The database is built by running the numbered SQL files (parts 1 through 9) in order in the Supabase SQL editor. Each is safe to re-run. The app is a single index.html plus a supplier portal.html, with connection keys in the CFG block at the top of each. Keep your own keys when replacing a file with a newer version.
  • Not yet built. Automatic emailing of requests, scheduled nightly list loading, a customer-facing report page, and user and settings administration screens.
